ADHD-YOLO

EEG classification research prototype

Converts a child's resting-state EEG into time–frequency images and classifies them ADHD or Control with a transfer-learned CNN. Built to test whether that approach works.

It does not. Measured across five methods on 84 subjects, the classifier performs at chance. This interface shows what the pipeline computes and where the prediction falls against that measured uncertainty.

measured chance range

The shaded range is the 95% confidence interval measured for this model across 84 subjects: 0.397 to 0.641. A prediction inside it carries no information about the child.

Not usable for screening. Pooled AUC 0.52 with the interval above containing 0.5, and a permutation test at p = 0.31. Five methods — two image representations, two capacity settings and a 301-feature classical model — all land here. Any single prediction from this system should be read as a coin flip.

What the model was given

Measured biomarkers

Computed from the signal directly, independently of the network.

Theta/beta ratio at F3, F4 and Fz. Published range for this age group is roughly 1.5 to 3.5.
ConditionRatio

Event-related and behavioural measures (P300 latency and amplitude, omission and commission errors, reaction time) are shown as unavailable rather than estimated. The task recordings' trigger channel does not encode the stimulus conditions the source dataset describes, so these cannot be computed.

What this is for

A research instrument, not a clinical one. It does not diagnose ADHD, and no output from it should inform a decision about a child. ADHD diagnosis requires clinical assessment.

Dataset: Rohani et al. (2022), 108 children. Model: YOLOv8n-cls, ImageNet-pretrained, subject-wise five-fold cross-validation with a held-out test split.
